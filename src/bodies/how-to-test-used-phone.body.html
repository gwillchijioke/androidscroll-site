Five minutes at the meetup catches most used-phone faults. I run these same checks on my Tecno Spark 40 (HiOS, Android 15) before any phone becomes my daily driver, and the routine works the same on a seller’s table in a mall food court. This guide gives you the checks in meetup order, the dialer codes that help, and the three money rules that keep you safe on Jiji and Konga.

The 5-minute meetup checklist

  1. Dial *#06# and match the IMEI to the box, receipt, and SIM tray
  2. Watch the seller remove their Google account, then confirm none remains
  3. Enrol a finger, restart the phone, unlock with that finger on first boot
  4. Run full-screen white, black, red, green, and blue, then drag an icon into every corner
  5. Place a live call, record a voice clip, play a track at 80% and 100%
  6. Shoot each camera lens plus a 30-second walking video
  7. Charge on your own charger and cable while you watch the percentage climb
  8. Insert your SIM, load a page on mobile data, read the signal under SIM status
  9. Check the maker battery gauge where one exists; feel for heat at idle

If any check fails, pause the deal and say so plainly. A genuine seller waits. A seller who rushes you has given you your answer.

Before You Meet the Seller

Compare the same model across two or three other listings first. Marketplace prices are leads, not fixed retail, and ten minutes of comparison tells you the going rate for that model in that condition. A price far below the rest is not luck. It is the first fault report.

Chat before you call. Ask for the exact model, storage size, and reason for sale in writing, plus clear photos of the screen on and off. Ask for the IMEI photo too. A seller with a clean phone sends it. A refusal tells you to stop before you leave home.

Pack four things: your own charger and cable, your own SIM card with airtime and data, earphones for the mic pass, and bring a friend when possible. Tell the seller in advance that you will test the phone for a few minutes on arrival. Consent up front removes all friction at the table.

The 5-Minute Meetup Checklist

Run these in order. Each step needs under a minute, and the order matters: identity first, locks second, hardware third.

1. IMEI first. Open the Phone app and dial *#06#. Confirm the number on screen matches the box, the receipt, and the SIM tray. Run it through a trusted checker such as IMEI.info to confirm the phone is genuine and not blacklisted. A mismatch, or a refusal to share the IMEI, means walk away.

2. Account lock demo. Ask the seller to remove their Google account in front of you, then open Settings and confirm no account remains. A signed-in phone can lock you out after a reset through Factory Reset Protection, and that lock survives everything else you test. No removal, no sale.

3. Reboot plus fingerprint. Enrol one finger, restart the phone, and unlock with that finger on first boot. This catches weak modules that enrol fine and fail in daily use. Repeat with face unlock in the mall light where the phone offers it.

4. Screen and touch. Fill the screen with solid white, black, red, green, and blue using a free screen test app or a test video, and scan for dead pixels, stuck pixels, tint patches, and banding at low brightness. Then long-press an icon and drag it slowly along every edge and into each corner. If it drops in one spot, that area has a dead zone. Repeat the drag before you trust a failure: a dry finger fakes it once.

5. Call, mic, and speaker. Place a live call and listen for echo and a weak earpiece. Record a short voice clip, whispered then spoken, and play it back. Play a bass-heavy track at 80% volume and listen for rattle and crackle, then push to 100%. On dual-speaker phones, cover the bottom speaker: sound should still come from the earpiece, which confirms stereo.

6. Cameras. Shoot one photo on each rear lens plus the front camera and zoom into detail. Then record a 30-second clip while walking: constant judder means stabilisation is off or broken. Check the lens glass for scratches and dust trapped inside.

7. Port and charge. Plug in your own charger and cable and confirm the percentage climbs while you watch. Tilt the plug gently: a phone that only charges at one angle has a worn port, and a worn port on a used phone is a repair bill hiding inside the price.

8. SIM and signal. Insert your SIM, place a call, and load a page on mobile data. A drop to No Service where other phones hold signal points at the modem or antenna. Read the exact figure under Settings > About phone > SIM status and compare it against your own phone in the same spot.

9. Heat at idle. Hold the phone after your five minutes of testing. Warm from work is normal. Hot while doing nothing is not. A used phone that runs hot at idle has a battery or board problem no discount fixes.

Dialer Codes That Help at the Meetup

Codes speed up the table test, but each one is undocumented: it may open a test menu or do nothing. A dead code proves nothing. The checklist above covers the same hardware by hand.

Samsung sellers get a first-party route with no codes: Samsung Members > Diagnostics > Phone diagnostics, then Test all. Run it at the table and watch each result land. Everyone else uses the manual passes above, which test the same parts with no menu at all.

Battery Truth on a Used Phone

Battery is where used phones lie most, so read it twice. First, open the maker gauge where one exists: Samsung Members > Support > Phone diagnostics > Battery status, or Settings > Battery > Battery health on Pixels that show it. On a readable gauge, anything short of Normal means walk away.

Second, ignore *#*#4636#*#* as a health readout. It shows battery level, not battery health, and no level number proves a strong cell. Where no gauge exists, run the 15-minute demanding game session from the guide: same game, same brightness, same settings. A phone that repeats its result across two sessions is probably fine. One that collapses on run two goes back to the seller.

If the phone drains fast after you take it home, work through the battery drain guide before you blame the cell: a rogue app looks exactly like a tired battery in week one.

Money Rules: Place, Return, Price

Three rules govern every meetup, and they hold on Jiji, on Konga meetups, and at Computer Village stalls alike.

Meet in the open. Never follow a seller down a back street, into a back room, or to a second location you did not agree. Choose a mall, a cafe, or somewhere with people and cameras, in daylight, and set the place yourself. A seller who dictates a lonely spot has told you everything.

Agree the return window before money moves. Seven days is fair: long enough for a weak battery or a hidden fault to show, short enough for any honest seller to accept. Say it out loud and keep it in chat. On Konga, eligible items carry a Guaranteed 7-Day Return Policy, so read the product page terms before you pay there.

If the price falls by half the moment you hesitate, walk away. A genuine seller holds the price. A collapsing price means the seller is offloading a problem, and the discount never covers the repair. The same holds in reverse: never send a transfer before you hold the phone. Pay after the test, in person, with the tested unit in your hand.

Final Word

At home, run the full new-phone checklist on it inside your return window: the 12 checks cover sensors, storage, and heat in depth, and anything odd you find still qualifies for a swap.
