I test every Android phone the same way before it becomes my daily driver: 12 checks, about 10 minutes for the fast pass. I ran each step in this guide on my Tecno Spark 40 (HiOS, Android 15). New owners start at the top; used-phone buyers run the fast pass, then jump to the used-phone fork below.

The 12 checks (10-minute pass)

  1. Body, screen glass, buttons, ports, and SIM tray under bright light
  2. Clean setup: skip data migration, Wi-Fi only, install the day-one patch
  3. Display: full-screen white, black, red, green, and blue
  4. Touch: drag an icon slowly along every edge and into each corner
  5. Dialer codes for your brand, or the no-code fallback below
  6. Speaker at 80% and 100%, three mic passes, haptics per keypress
  7. Every camera lens plus a 30-second walking video
  8. Proximity, light sensor, compass, and 360-degree rotation
  9. 15-minute demanding game: heat, frames, and battery together
  10. SIM calls and mobile data, signal reading, NFC tap
  11. Storage: Settings → Storage → System, compared against the same model
  12. Fingerprint plus spares, face in three lightings

If any check fails, stop setup and contact your seller: a fault found inside the return window is an easy swap.

The 12 Checks, in Order

1. Physical inspection. Before you power the phone on, hold it under bright direct light. Look for screen scratches, frame dents, uneven glass edges, and gaps between display and body. Press power and volume and confirm each click feels firm. Check the port, SIM tray, and speaker grills, plus the camera glass for dust or scratches. A speck trapped inside the lens is a factory defect. Ask for a swap straight away.

2. Minimal setup. Skip data migration when the wizard offers it. A clean phone tells the truth: lag or heat on a clean install points at hardware, not at an old app you carried over. Connect to Wi-Fi only, sign in to Google for Play Store access, and skip biometrics for now. They get a dedicated step at check 12. Two paths differ by brand. Adaptive brightness sits at Settings → Display & touch → Adaptive brightness on Pixel, usually Settings → Display → Adaptive brightness on Samsung and Tecno skins. Turn it off and push brightness to 100%. Then install any day-one patch under Settings → System → Software updates or System Update.

3. Display. Cycle solid white, black, red, green, and blue full-screen with a free screen test app or a YouTube test video. By common industry usage, a dead pixel stays black and a stuck pixel freezes on one colour. Google publishes no official method for this. One qualifier: backlight bleed applies to LCD panels only. OLED panels, which every Pixel and most 2026 flagships use, have no backlight and cannot bleed. On OLED look for grey or green tint and vertical banding at low brightness instead.

4. Touch. Long-press an icon and drag it slowly along every edge and into each corner. If it drops in one spot, that area may have a dead zone. No maker documents this method, so repeat the pass before you believe a failure. A screen protector or a dry finger can fake it once.

5. Dialer codes. Open the Phone app and try your brand code from the table below. Each code is undocumented: it may open a test menu or do nothing. If every code fails, the no-code fallback under the table covers the same hardware by hand.

6. Speaker, mic, and haptics. Play a bass-heavy Afrobeats track at 80% volume and listen for chassis rattle, crackle, and weak bass. On dual-speaker phones cover the bottom speaker; sound should still come from the earpiece, which confirms stereo. Then push to 100%. For mics: one call for echo, one Recorder clip whispered then spoken, one video near a fan. For haptics: fast typing should give a short tap per key, not a long buzz.

7. Cameras. Shoot textured fabric on the main sensor and zoom to 100%; detail should look crisp, not mushy. Then record a 30-second clip while walking: constant judder means stabilisation is off or broken.

8. Sensors. Cover the top of the screen during a call and the display should go dark. Switch auto-brightness on and shine a light near the front camera; the brightness should climb. Open a compass app or 360-degree video and rotate; movement should feel smooth. Note the split: the figure-eight gesture calibrates the compass (magnetometer), not the gyroscope. Gyroscope faults show as drift in 360-degree video instead.

9. Battery and heat. Play one demanding game for 15 minutes on high graphics and watch heat, frames, and battery together. Treat app readings as rough guides, not maker specs: Google and Samsung publish no temperature bands. I score nothing by fixed percentages, because drain depends on capacity, chipset, game, and brightness, and no maker publishes a pass mark. Run the same session twice under identical settings. A phone that repeats its result is probably fine. The one that collapses on run two is the one I return. A phone that slows charging while hot is doing what Pixels are documented to do: limit charge to protect the battery. For built-in gauges: Samsung Members → Support → Phone diagnostics → Battery status; Pixel Settings → Battery → Battery health, which shows Normal or Reduced, never Good. Several models show no status at all, including Pixel 6a and the 7 and 8 lines. On a new phone with a readable gauge, anything short of Normal goes back. If drain stays fast after setup, work through the battery drain guide before you blame the cell.

10. SIM, signal, Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, NFC. Insert each SIM, place a call, and load a page on mobile data. A drop to No Service where other phones hold signal points at the modem or antenna. Read dBm under Settings → About phone → SIM status. For NFC expect a read within a finger width. If a strange bug survives all of this, check the problems and fixes guide to confirm it is hardware before you return the phone.

11. Storage. Open Settings → Storage → System. There is no valid percentage rule, since the system image is a fixed GB size that swings widely by brand, so compare GB used against the same model. Install pending updates first; what remains is mostly the maker’s image size. A factory reset wipes user data only; it never re-partitions storage, so it cannot shrink the system image.

12. Fingerprint and face unlock. On Pixel, enrol under Settings → Security & privacy → Device unlock → Fingerprint Unlock, and face under the Face & Fingerprint Unlock entry. Pixel allows up to four fingerprints; other skins move these entries. Register a main finger plus spares, then try each from several angles. No maker publishes a speed spec, so judge as opinion: a good sensor feels near-instant and shrugs at slight misalignment, while repeated rejections point at a poor module. Enrol the face in even light, then test in daylight, indoor, and low light.

Dialer Codes and the No-Code Fallback

The codes below appear in no official maker documentation. A dead code proves nothing: fall back to Test Your Android, Phone Doctor Plus, or Device Info, or to the manual checks in this guide.

Samsung owners get a first-party route with no codes at all: Samsung Members → Diagnostics → Phone diagnostics, then Test all. The app walks through touch, speaker, camera, sensors, and more, with on-screen instructions per test. That is Samsung’s documented path. Everyone else is covered by hand: the colour cycle and icon drag (checks 3 and 4), the track and mic passes (check 6), the camera and sensor routines (checks 7 and 8), the maker’s battery gauge (check 9), and the dBm plus System GB readings (checks 10 and 11).

Buying Used: the Extra Fork

A used phone needs everything above plus proof it is clean, unlocked, and honestly described. Meet in a public place, carry your own charger and cable, and tell the seller in advance that you will inspect the phone. A genuine seller agrees. A seller who rushes you is giving you your answer.

IMEI first. Dial *#06# and confirm the number on screen matches the box, the receipt, and the SIM tray. Run it through a trusted checker such as IMEI.info to confirm the phone is genuine and not blacklisted. A mismatch, or a refusal to share the IMEI, means walk away.

Account lock demo. Ask the seller to remove their Google account in front of you, then confirm none remains. A signed-in phone can lock you out after a reset through Factory Reset Protection. No removal, no sale.

Reboot, biometrics, battery. Enrol a finger, restart the phone, and unlock with that finger on first boot. This catches weak modules that enrol fine and fail in real use. Then run the screen colours, a live call, and a charge cycle on your own charger while you watch. Check battery health through the maker’s Settings gauge where one exists; where none exists, the 15-minute game session from check 9 is your evidence. Anything short of Normal on a readable gauge, or a phone that runs hot doing nothing, goes back to the seller. See the used-phone meetup checklist for the full 9-step table routine.

Charger and Cable Test

Plug in your own charger and cable and confirm the percentage climbs while you watch. Where your engineering menu shows a charging current readout, compare your charger against a known-good one. A phone that only charges at one angle has a worn port, and a worn port on a used phone is a repair bill hiding inside the price.

Final Word

If your phone clears all 12 checks, set it up fully: migrate data, install apps, enable biometrics. You checked inside your seller’s window, so any hidden fault showed while a swap was still easy.
